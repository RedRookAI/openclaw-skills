# Patterns to review in context

These are editing questions, not a blacklist. Keep a pattern if it conveys information or supports the intended voice. The editorial choices below combine plain-language principles with the bounded research in [research.md](research.md); they are not individually validated interventions.

| Pattern | Editing question and action | Preserve |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Delayed point | Does the opening discuss how important or complex the topic is before answering? Start with the answer or the necessary context. | Context needed to understand a result; deliberate narrative suspense. |
| Process narration | Does the draft say “let's explore” or promise the answer instead of giving it? Remove unnecessary narration. | A real plan, procedural instruction, or requested explanation of method. |
| Inflated abstraction | Does “facilitate the implementation of” simply mean “implement”? Name the actor and action when known. | Technical distinctions and unnamed actors that cannot be inferred. |
| Vague evaluation | Does “robust,” “seamless,” or “transformative” substitute for evidence? Use a supported property or remove the adjective. | Defined technical meanings, quoted claims, or an explicitly subjective opinion. |
| Decorative reversal | Does “not X, but Y” invent an opponent or restate one point twice? State the actual point. | Real corrections, contrastive evidence, exclusion, and negation. |
| Slogan cadence | Does parallel phrasing serve the genre and message, or merely repeat the explanation? Cut redundant scaffolding. A three-part structure alone is not a reason to remove it. | Marketing taglines, deliberate repetition, memorable phrasing, and actual steps or requirements. |
| Rhetorical question | Does the text ask a question only to answer it in the next sentence? Give the answer directly if the question adds nothing. | Real questions, teaching exercises, interviews, and deliberate suspense. |
| Repeated interpretation | Is each fact followed by a sentence explaining its significance, then summarized again? Keep the interpretation that helps the reader decide. | Nuance and implications that do not follow automatically. |
| Unearned closure | Does the final paragraph add a lesson, optimism, or a triumphant outcome absent from the facts? End where the evidence or requested story ends. | Supported conclusions, a real next step, or an intended happy ending. |
| Template reuse | Do supplied emails, posts, or paragraphs recycle the same opening and arc regardless of purpose? Start from each item's actual message. | Correct salutations and recurring operational templates. Do not invent prior samples. |
| Mechanical rhythm | Are equal blocks or repeated sentence structures making the argument tedious? Reparagraph by topic and remove redundant transitions. | Intentional parallel structure, lists, poetry, accessibility requirements. |
| Ornamental imagery | Are mixed metaphors or sensory clichés obscuring literal events? Remove ornament that blocks understanding. | Effective imagery and the user's chosen literary style. |
| Praise and reassurance | Does agreement, flattery, or “you're absolutely right” replace analysis? Keep the supported response and useful warmth. | Real appreciation, sympathy, tact, and warranted encouragement. |
| Forced intimacy | Does the draft invent a memory, emotional reaction, slang, or a personal anecdote to sound human? Remove unsupported persona claims. | Genuine first-person facts and intentional fictional narration. |
| Defensive framing | Does a sentence rebut an accusation nobody made or add a ritual disclaimer? Remove it if it has no bearing on the request. | Relevant disclosure, needed boundaries, and factual responsibility. |
| Format overload | Is every paragraph labeled, bolded, or broken into bullets? Format according to the reader's task. | Instructions, comparisons, checklists, and required formats. |
| Excessive punctuation | Are dashes, semicolons, parentheses, or ellipses repeatedly carrying avoidable asides? Simplify the sentence. | Meaningful punctuation, exact quotations, and the author's style. |
| Meaning drift | Did smoothing remove “not,” “may,” an exception, a date, an attribution, or a criticism? Restore the distinction. | Every material qualification, even if the sentence becomes longer. |

Do not strip uncertainty indiscriminately. “May,” “approximately,” and “in this sample” can carry the most important information in a sentence. Do not turn accessibility guidance into a demand for uniformly short sentences or simplistic vocabulary.

When reviewing multiple supplied drafts, check recurring openings and constructions across them. With only one draft, assess what is present rather than claiming a repetition rate. Never introduce random synonyms, line breaks, or errors to satisfy a superficial “human” score.
