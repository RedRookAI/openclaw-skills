# AI Fiction: Pro Editor: before and after

**BEFORE = deliberately bad text supplied to the editor. AFTER = the actual edited result.**

Two invented demonstration scenes, edited by GPT-6.1-Sol using AI Fiction: Pro Editor. The AFTER passages are the actual model outputs.

## Fantasy: hiding from the guards

### BEFORE: deliberately bad input

Tavi was in the alley and the alley was narrow and it was raining rain which was wet on her face, reminding her that she was indeed standing outside in the rain. The rain was a symphony of liquid uncertainty. It cascaded down in a relentless tapestry of watery droplets—cold, cruel, merciless. She felt worried because the guards were looking for them, and being found by the guards would mean they had been found.

Orren held the sealed map case tightly in his hand in a tight grip. The case represented possibility. It embodied hope. It symbolized a future neither of them could yet begin to imagine.

"As you know, Tavi, you are my younger sister and we have stolen this map case from the duke," Orren said cautiously, raising his eyebrows cautiously. "The guards are searching for us because the duke owns the map case which we stole."

"I am aware," Tavi replied knowingly, nodding her head. "It's not about stealing a map—it's about reclaiming our destiny. We must leverage the opportunity to navigate our way out."

Footsteps sounded around the corner. Tavi heard the footsteps with her ears, which meant someone was walking somewhere close to where they currently were. She became more worried than she had previously been worried.

"There is a passage under the kitchens," she stated informatively, gesturing a gesture toward the low door. "We can utilize it."

"A passage? Under the kitchens?" Orren asked questioningly. "I did not know about this passage. Can we trust it?"

"We can trust it to be a passage," she quipped with a wry smirk that was both witty and profound. "And those footsteps are getting closer."

He hesitated hesitantly. She grabbed the handle and opened the door open. She went in first and he followed her in afterward. No guarantees. No surrender. Just courage. Their journey had only just begun.

### AFTER: actual Sol edit

Rain ran down Tavi’s face. The alley was narrow, with little room to pass Orren if he kept standing in her way. Her brother gripped the sealed map case they’d stolen from the duke. She wished he would tuck it out of sight. If the guards found them, they wouldn’t have to look far for proof.

“We should wait,” Orren murmured.

“Here?”

“Give them time to move on.”

Tavi glanced toward the mouth of the alley. Waiting gave the guards time to turn down it, too.

Footsteps sounded around the corner. She caught Orren’s sleeve and pointed to the low door.

“There’s a passage under the kitchens. We can use it.”

He looked at the door. “How do you know?”

“I know the way. Come on.”

“Wait. Is it safe?”

The footsteps were getting closer. Tavi strained to hear a second pair beneath the patter of rain, but couldn’t tell.

“It gets us off the street.”

Orren hesitated, the case held against his coat. She wanted him to agree, but every moment they stood there made her itch to move.

Tavi took the handle and pulled the door open. She ducked into the passage beneath the kitchens, and Orren followed her in.

## Contemporary fiction: dinner after an argument

### BEFORE: deliberately bad input

I was sitting at the kitchen table feeling sad, and the sadness I felt was a sad feeling of sadness. Our argument from earlier lingered like an invisible elephant dancing on eggshells in the room—a powerful testament to the fragile tapestry of human connection. The electricity was out, so the lights were not on, meaning the kitchen was dark because there was no electricity to power the lights.

Kit came back carrying a loaf of bread in a bag, highlighting their unwavering commitment to nourishment. They placed the bag on the table. They removed the bread from the bag. They looked at me across the table. They took a deep breath which was a breath they inhaled deeply.

"As you know, we are currently experiencing a power outage," Kit announced, their voice rich with unspoken emotion. "Therefore, the electric cooker cannot be used to cook our dinner, because it requires electricity."

"Yes," I said sadly, my sad eyes reflecting my sadness like sad mirrors. "It's not about the cooker—it's about whether we can weather the storms of life together."

Kit looked at the bread thoughtfully. They looked at the knife thoughtfully. They looked back at me thoughtfully. "I could make sandwiches," they said, offering a solution that was simple, practical, transformative.

I thought about how I was still angry. I was still angry because the argument had made me angry and I had not stopped being angry yet. Yet somewhere deep in the landscape of my heart, a beacon of hope began to ignite. Could sandwiches be the bridge that healed the chasm between us?

"All right," I replied with a gentle nod of reluctant acceptance, signaling my willingness to embark on the next chapter.

Kit cut two uneven slices. I moved my plate closer. We ate the cold sandwiches at the table in silence, a silence that spoke louder than words ever could. It wasn't a meal—it was a new beginning. Love. Resilience. Bread.

### AFTER: actual Sol edit

I sat at the kitchen table, feeling miserable. The electricity was out, and there was little to distract me from going over our argument again.

Kit came back with a loaf of bread in a bag. They set it on the table and unwrapped it, then glanced at me and took a breath.

“Cooker’s no use,” they said. “We’ll have to have something cold.”

“I know.” My voice came out sharper than I intended. “I’m still upset about earlier.”

Kit looked down at the bread. For a moment, neither of us said anything.

“I could make sandwiches,” they said.

I watched their hand rest beside the loaf. I was still angry, and I didn’t know what else to say to them. But I could sit here and eat.

“All right.”

Kit cut two uneven slices. I moved my plate closer and waited while they made the sandwiches.

We ate them cold, sitting across from each other at the kitchen table. Neither of us spoke.
